Methodology

All charts are computed from the TOP500 lists published twice a year (June and November) on www.top500.org, starting with the November 1994 list.

New entries in the top 10

A machine is counted the first time it appears in the top 10. Upgrades of the same system (same site, same name) are not counted again. The series starts with the November 1994 list, so its ten machines all appear as new. The shaded band shows the range from the #10 to the #1 of each list.

Performance of the list

For each list, four statistics of the measured performance (Rmax, LINPACK) of its 500 systems, on a log scale: the aggregate performance (sum of the 500 Rmax), the maximum (the #1 system), the mean, the median and the minimum (the #500 system). The gap between mean and median shows how much the list is dominated by its largest systems.

Everyday devices

This chart places a few consoles, phones and computers at their release date and at the peak performance of their graphics processor, against the #1 and #500 systems of each TOP500 list. A dot on the #1 curve, in the colour of the device and pointed at by a small arrow with its name, marks when the #1 system reached the same performance; on hover, the chart also tells until which list the device would have entered the TOP500.

The comparison is generous to the devices and only gives an order of magnitude. Their figures are theoretical single-precision (FP32) peaks, as quoted by manufacturers or usual technical sources, while TOP500 figures are measured double-precision (FP64) LINPACK results; the double-precision performance of these devices is often several times to tens of times lower. Some figures are estimates, since Apple does not publish the performance of its phone GPUs. The list of devices is in everyday_devices.py.

Power consumption

The TOP500 lists give the power of a system (in kW, measured during the LINPACK run or estimated) from June 2008 only, and not for every system: depending on the list, between about 40% and 90% of the 500 systems report it. The chart shows, among the systems that report it, the largest consumer, the #1 system, the mean, the median and the smallest consumer. The total of the reported values depends on how many systems report one, so it is only given on hover, with the number of systems concerned. Where the #1 system does not report its power, its line is interrupted.

The “power of new top 10 entries” chart places each machine entering the top 10 for the first time at the height of its reported power, with the same symbols as the first chart. Machines that do not report their power are not shown (about a quarter of the entries since June 2008).

Vendors

The TOP500 files have no structured “processor” field: the CPU vendor and the accelerator vendor are inferred from the machine description (“HPE Cray EX255a, AMD 4th Gen EPYC 24C 1.8GHz, AMD Instinct MI300A…”) with keyword rules. Self-hosted Xeon Phi (Knights Landing) count as CPUs, Xeon Phi cards (Knights Corner) as accelerators. Grace Hopper chips count for NVIDIA on both sides. Small families are grouped together; the detail appears on hover.

New processors and accelerators

A catalogue of about one hundred processor and accelerator generations (Xeon Sandy Bridge, EPYC Rome, Fermi, Hopper, SPARC64 VIIIfx…) is searched for in the description of every machine. The dot is placed at the first list in which at least one system uses it. Its height is the peak double-precision (FP64) performance of one device (log scale), its colour is the vendor, and its size reflects the peak number of systems using it in a single list. Technologies already present in the first list are drawn as outlines: they may be older than 1994.

Processors and accelerators each have their own chart. Accelerators are grouped like in the accelerator share chart (Cell and NEC SX-Aurora under “Others”). For the 1990s, descriptions give the machine model rather than the chip: ORIGIN 2000 is counted as MIPS R10000, SuperDome as PA-RISC, etc.

Performance of one device

The TOP500 lists only give totals per machine (Rmax, Rpeak, number of cores), not the number of chips, so the performance of a single processor or accelerator cannot be derived from them. The heights of the “new processors” and “new accelerators” charts therefore come from vendor specifications, listed in tech_catalog.py: the peak FP64 performance of one chip (or one package for multi-die parts such as the MI250X), for the model used by the first TOP500 system with that technology when it can be identified. For GPUs and accelerators, the vector FP64 figure is used, without matrix or tensor units. For the vector machines of the 1990s, one device is one processor or processing element. Chips without a reliable public FP64 figure (Hygon DCU, MetaX) are not plotted.

These values are approximate. The exact model is not always given in the TOP500 description, and a single generation covers several models with different clocks and core counts, so the true value of the first system may differ by a few tens of percent. Some values are more uncertain:

  • Radeon / FirePro (368 GFlop/s): Radeon HD 4870 X2 board at the reduced clock of Tianhe-1 (575 MHz instead of 750 MHz, 480 GFlop/s nominal); sources also disagree on the FP64 rate of this card.
  • Blackwell B200 (37 TFlop/s): figure of the HGX / DGX B200 (1000 W); the B200 of the GB200 reaches about 45 TFlop/s.
  • Hopper (26 TFlop/s): figure of the H100 PCIe used by the first system; the H100 SXM reaches 34 TFlop/s.
  • Data Center GPU Max 1550 (52 TFlop/s): Intel's peak FP64 figure; sources disagree on whether it is a vector or matrix rate, and give vector peaks between about 26 and 30 TFlop/s.
  • Instinct MI325X / MI355X (78.6 TFlop/s): MI355X figure; the MI325X is rated at 81.7 TFlop/s.
  • EPYC Turin (9.8 TFlop/s): assumes full-width AVX-512 (32 FP64 operations per cycle and core) at base clock; actual sustained clocks under AVX-512 are lower.
  • ClearSpeed (50 GFlop/s): figure for one Advance accelerator board (two chips), not one chip.
  • GRAPE-DR (256 GFlop/s) and MN-Core (32.8 TFlop/s): figures announced by the designers, with little independent documentation.
  • PEZY-SC (1.5 TFlop/s): depends on the clock of the first systems (about 690–733 MHz).
  • LX2 (60.3 TFlop/s): figure given by its designers for LineShine, which counts the matrix units of the chip; its vector FP64 performance is not published and is probably much lower.
  • Hitachi SR8000 (1 GFlop/s) and SR11000 (13.6 GFlop/s): Hitachi quotes figures per node; the per-processor value is derived from the node figure.
  • Pre-2000 RISC and vector processors: clock rates vary between models of the same machine, and the first system's clock is often not given; the value uses a typical clock of the time.

Shares

In “number of systems” mode, each chart gives the percentage of the 500 machines. In “share of performance” mode, it gives the fraction of the list’s aggregate Rmax. In the accelerator chart, the top of the stack is therefore the share of accelerated systems; the rest are CPU-only.

Limits

Because vendors and technologies are inferred from free text, some systems may be misclassified, especially in the oldest lists. These groupings are an interpretation made for this infographic and do not come from the TOP500 project.

Reproducing the data

The data are produced by build_infographic_data.py from the TOP500 XML files; the technology catalogue is in tech_catalog.py.
